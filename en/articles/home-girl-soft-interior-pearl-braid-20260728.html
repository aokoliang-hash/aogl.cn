This is a personal character-line archive on aogl.cn. Every asset lives under original/home-girl/ (dated 2026-07-28). The line stays deliberately soft and domestic: a cream ribbed camisole, a matching sheer shirt worn off the shoulders, a dark side braid, a fine pearl necklace, pale wood floors, and white walls with leaf-window shadows. It is not fantasy couture and not an urban knit-shorts set—it runs beside girl1, girl2 Lyra, and role-girl to answer one question: under quiet indoor daylight, can the same person survive a nine-cell sheet, necklace macros, and a short video without identity drift? This page is not a prompt cookbook. It walks filenames: bust anchors, a contact sheet, pearl close-ups, floor sits with a Canon prop, and video1.mp4 motion QA.

Why a separate home-girl folder

girl1 owns city-window beige knit shorts; girl2 owns lavender butterfly ceremony; role-girl owns white lace card poses. Stuffing “indoor cream camisole + pearl + side braid” into any of those folders would tangle wardrobe meaning and filenames. home-girl is a batch label—“home face line”—while public titles spell out soft interior / ribbed camisole / side braid / pearl so crawlers can index long-tails like “home aesthetic character sheet,” “clean girl portrait consistency,” and “window light pearl necklace study.”

For SEO I repeat entities in prose: home-girl, cream ribbed camisole, side braid, pearl necklace, leaf-window light, pale wood floor, 3×3 contact sheet, video1.mp4. HTML ships first; naked wallpaper rarely ranks design queries.

Identity rules: five checks that beat “pretty”

  1. Hair — dark middle part; one thick braid over the left shoulder; light hair tie at the tip.
  2. Wardrobe ladder — cream ribbed camisole as base; matching open shirt may slip off shoulders, never mutates into a dark blazer.
  3. Necklace anchor — fine pearl chain with a center pendant (heart or single large pearl variants); collarbone must stay readable.
  4. Light signature — white wall + leaf or mullion shadows (komorebi feel), warm daylight.
  5. Set dressing — pale wood floor, shelves, botanical hangings, folded textiles; optional Canon body as a “home studio” prop that must not steal the face.

If a new frame widens the jaw, swaps to twin tails, drops the necklace, or jumps to neon street night, it stays out of the keep set.

Contact sheet: indoor QA board

z76km6… packs nine cameras onto one cream void: front, three-quarter, profile, low necklace angle, high braid-touch, back glance, eye macro, braid tip, collarbone pendant. Like girl1’s grids, it is a private QA board, not a social collage. The eye cell catches iris and lash drift across regenerations; the back cell checks whether straps and braid root still belong to the same person.

Writing cell intents into HTML matters for search: “side braid profile contact sheet,” “pearl necklace eye macro,” and “home aesthetic turnaround grid” become indexable even if a crawler never OCRs the PNG.

Mid shots: will the room eat the face?

Environment frames place her in a readable home—wood shelves, folded throws, leaf tapestries, small ceramics. I need to know whether the set eats the face: pale wood bounce washing skin gray, tapestry lines turning into thumbnail noise. The palms-on-floor sit (hs96tp…) checks shoulder line and shirt slip stability; the braiding process frame (nz0s0s…) checks whether bangs still match mid-action.

Pearl macros: small props need a mini bible

Home lines lose necklaces first in thumbnails. o1rkmq… pushes collarbone, rib texture, and a single pearl into the foreground; vp843f… builds a triptych—center jewelry, side wall shadow and shelf blur—to remind me that accessory macros are identity-part QA, not ecommerce PDPs. Heart pendants and larger single pearls may vary within the batch, but chain type and wear height should stay consistent.

Low angle and the camera prop

dtnski… is a floor-level still: one hand on the braid, one on the boards, a soft-focus black Canon body in front, a classical portrait painting behind. It answers two questions—(1) will shallow depth of field melt the face into a stranger; (2) will the “creative prop” outrank the person. I keep the camera silhouette readable but claim no brand partnership; it is set dressing for a home-studio story.

video1.mp4: motion QA after stills

Stills can cheat with single-frame sharpness; short video reveals braid tip flicker, camisole edges, and whether the open shirt tracks the shoulders. video1.mp4 is one retained motion pass—not a commercial deliverable. Watch it against the nine-cell sheet: if the jaw widens mid-move or the necklace strobes away, rerun from the bust anchor instead of hand-fixing the clip.

For SEO, pairing a real video element, a descriptive poster, and prose that names the file beats uploading a naked MP4 with no context—the same pattern used on the role-girl motion notes.

What I deliberately skipped

There is no interactive HTML demo, no skeletal walk cycle, and no commercial texture pack. Assets are personal practice, not fashion-brand or camera-vendor IP. Leaf shadows and room props are generative look-dev—not claimed as a real showroom shoot.

I also refuse to mash the nine-cell sheet and necklace macros into one collage hero. Collages look noisy in Open Graph previews and hide which file answers which QA question. The cover WebP comes from the environment bust via npm run generate-article-covers.

If you copy this folder structure

Recommended order: one environment bust anchor → one face close-up ruler → nine-cell contact sheet → necklace macro → sit / process frames → one short video QA. Write what question each file answers. That reads more like an original production note than eight hundred empty “aesthetic” adjectives—and it ranks better for concrete queries such as soft interior character sheet, side braid pearl portrait, and home aesthetic AI consistency.

Do not open a home-girl-2 folder until this keep set is stable. Folder sprawl without a locked face ruler is how identity debt accumulates on disk and in search snippets.
