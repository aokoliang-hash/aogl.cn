Reading Steam Charts: bestsellers, Most Played, and weekly tops — personal notes

Official Valve Charts · not a commercial ranking · weekly snapshot

2026-06-22 · aogl.cn original

This is game editorial #1 on aogl.cn: personal notes on reading Steam Charts inside Valve’s store—not a third-party stat site, not a “must play” list. The hub lives under the store nav item Charts, with Most Played sorting by live concurrent players and separate views for revenue leaders. I compare official wording with my Sunday habit on games.html; I do not mirror key art or paste store descriptions—only link out to Steam app pages.

Three charts, three questions

New readers often collapse “popular” into one idea. Steam splits at least these lanes—I open Charts after skimming Hot mix RSS to answer different questions:

  • Top Sellers — “Who is paying this week?” Pre-orders, seasonal sales, and DLC spikes show up here. This is revenue momentum, not concurrent headcount.
  • Most Played — “Who is running the client right now?” Rows expose live concurrents and peaks. Free-to-play staples and esports titles dominate; that is a different story from a day-one single-player launch.
  • Weekly top sellers — “What sold best in this seven-day window?” Useful for launch week vs. evergreen MMO concurrency—closer to publishing cadence than minute-by-minute online counts.

Official hub: store.steampowered.com/charts/. Most Played deep link: …/charts/mostplayed. I do not treat Charts as an APK discovery feed; it is for readers already inside the PC ecosystem.

Most Played: live, but not a quality score

The appeal of Most Played is near-real-time motion: patch days, weekend evenings, and tournament slots move numbers quickly. Valve publishes platform-side stats—not Metacritic, not my taste. Before I over-read a row I ask:

  1. Is this a permanent F2P tenant (Counter-Strike 2, Dota 2) or a patch pulse on a premium game?
  2. Which time zone’s night drives the peak I am seeing? (I fetch US-facing charts; your locale may differ.)
  3. Does high concurrency imply “buy it”?—Usually no; it only means “many people are in-session now.”

So this article keeps a weekly snapshot table (below), not a fake live ticker. For minute-level truth, use Valve’s page directly.

Top Sellers: revenue is not concurrency

The bestseller list tracks store transactions: launches, bundles, Deck-verified bumps, and winter sale waves reshuffle order constantly. Seeing game A on Most Played while game B tops sellers is normal—evergreen online titles vs. a discounted new release is the classic split.

I use Top Sellers to cross-check first-party headlines on games.html: when PlayStation or Xbox blogs mention a title and Steam revenue also spikes, I open the app page for a patch or DLC—not to write “10/10 must buy.” See Games hub RSS × generative art workflow: sometimes RSS alone is enough without a new article.

Weekly tops: a changelog-friendly ruler

Weekly seller charts compress noise into a seven-day frame—easier for a static git site than pretending to stream live data. After npm run fetch-steam-charts I can commit JSON and note “weekly movers” in changelog without websocket infra. Weekly answers what sold; Most Played answers what is running.

Scope and compliance

  • No long store copy, no mirrored key art—only outbound /app/ links.
  • Not affiliated with Valve; no key reselling; no piracy indexes.
  • Snapshots are reading aids, not purchase advice.
  • Region, age gates, and account state change what you see; fetches default to US context.

Can this page auto-update every week?

Yes—for the snapshot table. Valve’s own Most Played numbers move continuously; this repo does not websocket-scrape them. Instead, fetch-steam-charts tries once per week (GitHub Actions or your cron), writes JSON, and build-articles injects fresh rows into this URL. That is the honest split: official Charts stay live; aogl.cn keeps a dated audit trail. If you need Tuesday-morning concurrency, open Most Played in another tab while reading this explainer.

When store JSON endpoints return 403 (common on some networks), add a Steam Web API key as repo secret STEAM_WEB_API_KEY so the workflow can call ISteamChartsService/GetMostPlayedGames and weekly seller services documented on partner portals. Without a key, the prose and SEO pills still stand; only the auto-table may stay empty until a successful fetch from GitHub’s US runners.

Maintenance
